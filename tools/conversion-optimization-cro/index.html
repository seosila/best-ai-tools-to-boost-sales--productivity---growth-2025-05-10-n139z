Conversion Optimization (CRO)

Conversion optimization is a learning process: find where people struggle, investigate why, and test a focused change against a measurable goal. Clarity, Hotjar, and VWO cover different parts of that work. They can complement one another, but they are not interchangeable—and collecting more behavior data does not, by itself, improve a page.

Practical workflow: from observation to improvement

Read our guide to using heatmaps and session recordings for a step-by-step process that connects analytics, behavior research, privacy checks, and experiment design.

Three different CRO jobs

1. Understand what visitors do

Behavioral analytics uses aggregated heatmaps and individual session recordings to reveal clicks, scrolling, navigation paths, and possible friction. This can identify where to investigate, but a replay or heatmap is evidence to interpret—not proof of why a visitor acted.

2. Investigate why they struggle

Surveys, feedback prompts, and user research add visitor explanations and context. Use specific questions at an appropriate point in the journey, and consider interviews or usability sessions when a survey response cannot explain the underlying task or expectation.

3. Test whether a change helps

Experimentation assigns eligible traffic to alternatives and measures a chosen outcome. A/B tests can estimate whether a change affected that outcome, but need a sound hypothesis, reliable measurement, and enough suitable traffic for a useful result.

Choose for your situation

Traffic and measurement

If traffic is low, an A/B test may take a long time to distinguish a real effect from ordinary variation. Avoid frequent peeking or declaring a winner from a handful of conversions. Start with analytics, usability checks, customer feedback, and fixing clear defects; test only when the expected traffic and outcome volume support a meaningful comparison.

Implementation and skills

Behavior tools generally require adding a tracking snippet or supported installation and then configuring goals, filters, and data collection. Experimentation may need engineering input for complex sites, event instrumentation, quality assurance, or changes beyond a visual editor. Map that work before comparing subscription prices.

Privacy and data handling

Session replay and feedback can involve sensitive page content or user-provided information. Review the vendor's data controls, masking, retention, access, and consent options; configure them before collecting data. Determine what notices, permissions, and retention practices your organization needs with its privacy or legal team. A vendor's privacy tools do not decide your obligations for you.

Budget and feature scope

Check current plan entitlements, traffic or session allowances, replay sampling, survey response limits, retention, projects, and experiment features. A free or entry-level analytics plan does not imply every research or experimentation capability is included. Compare the total cost of the products you actually need.

Compare the approaches

Tool Best fit Distinctive role Check before adopting
Microsoft Clarity Teams starting with behavioral analytics Heatmaps, session recordings, and AI-assisted summaries; official pricing currently lists Clarity as free. Consent, masking, data use, and privacy disclosure; it is not a dedicated A/B testing product.
Hotjar / Contentsquare Teams combining behavior analysis with visitor input or research Behavior analytics (heatmaps and replays), Voice of Customer (surveys and feedback), and User Research are distinct product areas. Hotjar URLs now lead to Contentsquare; plans, feature access, and usage limits vary by product and subscription.
VWO / Wingify Teams that want to run structured web experiments Web Experimentation includes A/B, multivariate, and split-URL tests; separate Behavior Analytics can add heatmaps and recordings. The current VWO URLs redirect to Wingify; analytics products and plan entitlements are modular, and some integrated analysis needs both products.

Clarity and Hotjar/Contentsquare primarily help teams discover and investigate behavior; VWO/Wingify is more directly focused on experimentation, with separate analysis products available. Pairing an analytics tool with an experiment platform can be complementary, while Clarity and Hotjar may overlap for basic recordings and heatmaps. Choose based on the research question and coverage you need, not feature-count alone.
