Website and app measurement

Google Analytics 4 (GA4)

Google Analytics 4 measures activity across websites and apps using events. Its reports can help teams understand acquisition, engagement, and configured conversion outcomes, making it useful when marketing teams need a broad view of where visitors come from and how they interact with digital properties.

Visit Google Analytics Read Analytics help

What it is suited to measure

GA4 combines website and app measurement in an event-based data model. Teams can use acquisition reports to examine traffic sources, engagement reporting to understand activity, and event or key-event reporting to assess actions they have identified as important. For example, a purchase, form submission, or account registration can be treated as a key event once tracking is set up and the event is marked accordingly.

GA4 may fit content publishers, ecommerce teams, and businesses seeking cross-site/app measurement and acquisition context. Its reports are most useful when the team has agreed on event definitions and configured tagging, app streams, attribution, and key events for its own goals.

Setup and limitations

Setup is not the same as complete business tracking. Google documents automatically collected events alongside recommended and custom events; important business actions may need an implementation, configuration, or verification step. Test the data and consent controls in your actual deployment, and avoid sending information your organization should not collect.

GA4's breadth can be less direct for teams whose main job is detailed product adoption, cohort behavior, or user-level journeys. For event-centered product analytics, compare Mixpanel and Amplitude. Review current Google terms and configuration options for your privacy, retention, and reporting requirements.
