Apollo vs Lusha: Features, Pricing, and Which Fits Your Sales Workflow

Apollo and Lusha both help B2B teams find business contacts, but they are not interchangeable. Apollo is a stronger fit to evaluate when you want prospecting and sales engagement in one system. Lusha is a stronger fit when contact discovery and enrichment need to work alongside a CRM and sales tools you already use. The right choice depends on your workflow, target-market coverage, and how each product's credit rules map to the data you actually reveal.

Last reviewed October 14, 2026 · Assessment based on vendor information; neither product was hands-on tested.

Apollo vs Lusha features

The practical difference is less about a universal winner and more about whether you want prospect data and outreach execution together, or a contact-data layer that feeds your established sales stack.

Contact and company discovery

Apollo's official prospecting materials describe searching for B2B prospects and accounts, researching them, and moving into sales workflows. Lusha's product materials emphasize business contact and company information, contact reveals, and enrichment. If your main task is building lists and then acting on them without moving between multiple tools, Apollo's wider workflow may be useful. If you have an outreach process already and need to identify or complete contact records, Lusha's focused data workflow may be more relevant.

Neither vendor's published contact totals establish how many usable records exist for your specific industry, country, job titles, or company size. Business details can be incomplete or outdated in any database. Run a small sample using real target accounts and check the fields you need before committing to a larger plan.

Enrichment, buying signals, and research

Apollo documents contact and account enrichment and AI-assisted prospect research. Its API materials also describe searching and enriching lead data and syncing sales objects such as tasks, sequences, and opportunities. Lusha describes contact and company enrichment and buying signals that can help teams decide when to review or prioritize an account. These capabilities address related but distinct jobs: adding context to a record, finding a reason to revisit an account, and researching a prospect are not the same as executing an outreach campaign.

Treat buying signals as prompts to investigate, not proof that a company is ready to buy. Confirm which signal types, refresh intervals, enrichment actions, and plan limits are included in the vendor's current offer.

Email outreach and sales engagement

This is a meaningful point of separation. Apollo describes a sales engagement stack for multichannel work across email, phone, and social, alongside tasks, account management, and meeting booking. It may reduce the need to assemble separate prospecting and engagement tools, though a team should check channel requirements, plan access, and how its existing process would change.

Lusha's core role is contact intelligence. Its integration marketplace lists connections to tools including Outreach and Salesloft, but a connection to an engagement platform does not make Lusha's data product equivalent to an outreach sequencer. If your team already uses a sales engagement system and only needs more relevant contact data, a focused data provider may avoid replacing that workflow.

CRM, integrations, automation, and APIs

Apollo describes its product as working with tools such as Salesforce and Chrome, and documents API use for prospect search, enrichment, and sales-object syncing. Lusha has a published integrations marketplace; its official setup pages document CRM workflows for Salesforce and HubSpot, including exporting contacts and companies and enriching records. Lusha also documents API access and other workflow connections. Specific capabilities can vary by integration and plan, so confirm field mapping, permissions, rate limits, and whether the integration supports the workflow you intend to run.

Adoption effort depends on your CRM data model, admin access, list hygiene, and the number of steps you automate. A browser extension or simple export may be straightforward to trial; bulk enrichment, API pipelines, and multi-system synchronization require more planning. That is a workflow consideration, not a claim that one product is inherently easier for every team.

Apollo vs Lusha pricing and credits

Neither vendor's credit should be treated as a shared unit of value. Compare the bill and the work completed for your own sample—not just the headline allowance. Plan names, features, and prices can change; use the vendors' current Apollo pricing page and current Lusha pricing page to confirm terms.

Apollo's credit model

Apollo describes plan-dependent credits for activities such as accessing email and phone data, AI-researched information, and other platform actions. Its pricing guidance says credits are pooled across the team, issued at the start of a billing cycle, reset at the next cycle, and do not roll over when unused. The precise cost depends on the action and plan; verify the current in-account credit details before projecting monthly usage.

Lusha's credit model

Lusha currently advertises a free plan with 40 credits per month, with paid tiers and allowances described on its pricing page. Its FAQ says a revealed verified email costs one credit and a revealed phone number five credits in the Workspace and extension; a company-information reveal and API requests have separate rules. Monthly paid-plan credits can roll over up to twice the monthly plan limit while subscribed, while annual-plan credits are issued upfront and unused credits reset at the end of the annual cycle. Check the current plan terms, since credit treatment depends on billing type and action.

These examples are not directly comparable: Apollo's credits can cover several product activities, while Lusha publishes different costs for specific data reveals and API operations. Build an estimate from your intended workflow—such as email-only prospecting, phone-led calling, CRM enrichment, or API-based lookups—and include seats, plan restrictions, and any credit expiration or rollover limits.

Which should you choose?

Apollo: advantages, trade-offs, and ideal use cases

Advantages: Prospecting, contact and account research, enrichment, and sales engagement are presented within one product family. That breadth can make sense when the team wants one place to find targets and coordinate outreach.

Trade-offs: The broader workflow can mean more features and configuration than a team needs if its only gap is missing contact details. Credit use spans different actions, and unused credits do not roll over under Apollo's published pricing guidance. Review the exact activities and limits your process will use.

Consider Apollo if: You are building an outbound workflow and want prospect discovery together with multichannel sales engagement. You may not need it if: Your team already has a prospecting and sequencing system and only needs a data source to enrich records.

Lusha: advantages, trade-offs, and ideal use cases

Advantages: Contact discovery and enrichment are central to the product, and official Salesforce and HubSpot integrations support moving data into existing CRM workflows. The published credit examples can help a small team model a narrow use case, provided it accounts for differing email, phone, and API actions.

Trade-offs: Credit needs can rise faster for phone-number reveals or larger enrichment runs. If you need a complete prospecting-to-sequencing workspace, Lusha's data focus may mean keeping or adding a separate engagement platform. Confirm that your CRM, target geographies, and required fields are covered before you pay.

Consider Lusha if: You already have a CRM and outreach process and want to evaluate a dedicated source for business contact data, enrichment, and signals. You may not need it if: Existing sources already provide the contact fields you use or your main need is managing outbound sequences rather than sourcing data.

Recommendations by team and situation

  • Solo founders and very small businesses: Start by defining the target customer and the number of contacts you will actually reach. A small Lusha credit allowance may suit occasional data lookup if you already handle outreach elsewhere; Apollo is worth evaluating if you need prospect search and engagement together. Trial the workflow against a short list before taking on a recurring subscription.
  • Small B2B sales teams: Favor Apollo when reps need a connected path from identifying prospects to organizing outreach and the team can monitor shared credit use. Favor Lusha when the CRM and sequencer are already established and the missing piece is contact discovery or enrichment.
  • Teams with a CRM and sales engagement platform already: Begin with Lusha's integration and data coverage for your CRM, then compare sample records and total credit use. Apollo can still fit if its data and engagement features would replace a meaningful part of the current stack, but avoid paying for overlapping capabilities without a migration reason.
  • Teams that want prospecting and outreach in one system: Apollo is the more direct candidate based on its documented multichannel sales engagement. Validate the available channels, plan limits, and CRM workflow in the current product before moving sequences or users.

A practical evaluation checklist

  1. Choose a representative sample of companies and job titles from your real target market, not a vendor-provided showcase list.
  2. Check whether each tool returns the email, phone, company, and signal fields you actually use. Mark missing or stale-looking records rather than assuming a match is current.
  3. For the same test workflow, record every credit-consuming action. Keep email reveals, phone reveals, enrichment, API lookups, and engagement actions separate.
  4. Estimate total cost for your expected seats and monthly activity. Account for Apollo's non-rollover guidance and Lusha's billing-type-specific rollover rules.
  5. Confirm your CRM integration, permissions, duplicate handling, export behavior, and any API limits with the actual administrator who will own setup.
  6. Decide whether you need data only or both data and outreach execution. Prefer the least overlapping setup that covers your process reliably.

Apollo vs Lusha FAQ

Is Apollo better than Lusha?

Not for every buyer. Apollo is a better fit to evaluate when you want prospecting and sales engagement together. Lusha is a better fit to evaluate when contact intelligence and enrichment need to feed a CRM or engagement workflow you already use. Verify both against your market and process rather than assuming one has better data overall.

Which is better for a small business?

It depends on what is missing. A very small business that wants to run prospecting and outreach from one system may prefer to trial Apollo's workflow. A small business with an existing CRM and sequencer may prefer to test Lusha for targeted contact lookups. Compare the cost of the actual actions you expect, not a nominal credit count.

Are Apollo and Lusha credits equivalent?

No. Apollo applies credits to multiple plan-dependent actions, while Lusha documents different credit costs for email, phone, company information, and API operations. The same numeric balance does not mean the same number or type of usable records. Check each vendor's current rules and estimate your workflow separately.

Can Lusha replace Apollo for sales outreach?

Lusha can supply contact intelligence and connect to sales engagement tools, but its core role is data and enrichment. Apollo documents sales engagement across email, phone, and social. If you use Lusha instead of Apollo, confirm that your current sequencer or other tools cover the execution tasks you need.

Can I use either product with my CRM?

Both document CRM-related workflows, but exact integrations, features, and permissions differ. Lusha publishes integration pages for Salesforce and HubSpot. Apollo documents working with Salesforce among its supported sales workflows. Confirm current feature availability, field mapping, and plan access for your CRM before rollout.

Which tool has more accurate contact data?

This comparison does not establish that either provider is more accurate. Data quality varies by market, role, and record, and information can go stale. Run a controlled sample on your target accounts and independently check a subset before deciding.
